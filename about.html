My Story

How a spreadsheet habit turned into a bookkeeping practice.

Every bookkeeper has an origin story, and mine starts the way a lot of them do: with a small business that needed its books cleaned up, and nobody else around to do it. What began as helping out turned into a genuine fascination with the way clean financial records change how a business owner sleeps at night. There is a particular kind of relief that comes from finally knowing, with certainty, what your numbers actually say — and I found I liked being the person who delivers that relief.

Samza Bookkeeping launched in 2019 out of the belief that small business owners deserve a bookkeeper who treats their books like they matter, not like a line item to be processed as quickly as possible. Since then, the practice has grown one referral at a time, working with e-commerce shops, service businesses, trucking companies, retail stores, and everything in between — proof that good bookkeeping principles hold up no matter what the business actually sells.

"Good books don't just tell you where you've been. They tell you what you can afford to do next."

What hasn't changed since day one is the structure of the practice itself: no account managers, no bouncing between staff, no outsourcing your books to whoever happens to be free that week. When you work with Samza Bookkeeping, you work with Sam — every reconciliation, every report, every phone call.

Outside of the day-to-day client work, I stay current through ongoing QuickBooks Online training and certification, because software changes fast and half-updated knowledge is how mistakes creep into a client's books. Staying sharp on the tools is part of the job, not an afterthought.

How I Work

A few principles that guide every engagement.

These aren't marketing language — they're the actual standards each client relationship gets held to.

01

Direct communication

No account managers or ticket queues. Questions get answered by the person who actually did the work.

02

Meticulous accuracy

Reconciliation isn't optional or occasional. Every account gets checked, every month, without exception.

03

Real partnership

Your business context matters. Numbers get interpreted with an understanding of how you actually operate.

04

Plain-English reporting

Financial statements should inform decisions, not require a translator. Reports are built to be read, not decoded.
